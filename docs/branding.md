# Lading — Brand Guidelines

> **Every document, right the first time.**

---

## 1. Brand Overview

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Brand name** | Lading |
| **Etymology** | From *"bill of lading"* — the core legal document in international trade. The name signals the one artefact every shipment depends on. |
| **Tagline** | Every document, right the first time. |
| **Positioning statement** | Your forwarder's paperwork is only as good as the data you hand over — we make it error-free before it leaves your desk. |
| **Category** | Trade documentation & compliance SaaS |
| **Primary market** | Nigeria (import and export), expanding to Rwanda, East Africa, and AfCFTA corridors |
| **Audience** | SME importers/exporters (self-serve) and professional clearing agents / freight forwarders (team seats) |
| **Personality** | Classy, contemporary, trustworthy, precise, calm, professional |

### What Lading is

A SaaS platform that captures a shipment once and turns it into a complete, compliant document set:

1. **Capture** a shipment a single time.
2. **Generate** import/export documents — commercial invoice, packing list, proforma, certificate of origin, bill of lading data.
3. **Determine** which documents and permits a shipment requires, from HS code + origin + destination + direction.
4. **Check** cross-document consistency to catch errors before submission.
5. **Advise** on Incoterms selection.
6. **Estimate** landed cost and duty.

### Elevator pitch

Lading is the documentation layer for African trade. It captures a shipment once, then generates the invoices, packing lists, certificates, and bill of lading data that importers, exporters, and clearing agents need — flagging the permits and consistency errors that cause delays before anything reaches a customs desk. Built in Nigeria for African trade corridors, Lading makes every shipment's paperwork right the first time.

## 3. Voice & Tone

**Voice:** assured, precise, unhurried. We speak like a senior compliance officer who respects your time. We never shout, never oversell, and never use jargon as a substitute for clarity.

**Tone principles**

- **State facts, not hype.** Numbers and specifics over adjectives.
- **Be calm under pressure.** Customs errors are stressful; our language never adds to that.
- **Respect the user's expertise.** Agents know trade — we add rigour, not lectures.
- **Be specific about the next step.** Always point to the action.

### Say this / Not that

| ✅ Say this | ❌ Not that |
|---|---|
| "Your packing list and invoice disagree on 3 line items. Review them before submission." | "Oops! Something went wrong with your documents!" |
| "This HS code requires a Form M and a Pre-Arrival Assessment Report." | "Get compliant fast with our amazing all-in-one solution!" |
| "Lading checks 40+ consistency rules across your document set." | "Revolutionary AI that does literally everything for you." |

## 4. Logo Direction

The wordmark should feel engineered and calm — the visual equivalent of a correctly stamped document. Concepts below deliberately avoid globes, handshakes, ships with swooshes, and generic "connection" dots.

### Concept A — "The Clean Line"

- **Idea / rationale:** The bill of lading is a ruled, columned form. The mark distils that form into a single, perfectly aligned set of lines — the moment when everything lines up. It reads as precision and quiet authority.
- **The mark:** A geometric monogram/symbol built from four to five horizontal rules of varying length, right-aligned to a strong vertical stem. The negative space between the lines suggests a column of document fields. Optionally, one line extends into a subtle checkmark terminal. Pairs with the wordmark "LADING" in a confident grotesque, with a slight tracking increase.

### Concept B — "The Seal"

- **Idea / rationale:** A customs seal or stamp is the moment a document becomes valid. The mark abstracts a seal into a precise geometric ring — a single break in the ring implies verification and progress. It signals trust and finality without looking bureaucratic.
- **The mark:** A circular stamp form drawn as a thin, even ring with one clean gap (a "verified" break), enclosing a small abstracted checkmark or a stylised "L" formed from two right angles. Sits well as an icon-only mark and reads clearly at favicon size. Wordmark set in a refined grotesque below or beside it.

### Concept C — "The Route"

- **Idea / rationale:** A shipment moves along a corridor — origin to destination. The mark shows two precise anchor points joined by a single confident path that resolves into a document edge. It communicates movement and structure without a literal map or globe.
- **The mark:** Two small geometric nodes (a circle and a square) connected by one straight-then-angled line that terminates in a short vertical bar, forming an abstracted shipping route that also resembles the corner of a page. Monoline, no fills, built on a strict grid. Wordmark "LADING" in a clean grotesque, horizontal lockup.

All values are **locked**. Do not introduce alternative hues.

| Name | Hex | CSS Variable | Intended usage | Ratio |

### Usage ratio (60 / 30 / 10)

| Share | Role | Colours |
|---|---|---|
| **60%** | Neutrals & canvas | Cloud `#F5F7FA`, white, Border `#E2E8F0` |
| **30%** | Navy foundation | Deep Harbor `#0B1F33`, Harbor Navy 700 `#123A5A`, Ink `#1F2937` |
| **10%** | Accent energy | Signal Teal `#0E9F8E` (~7%), Manifest Amber `#F2A93B` (~3%) |

Keep teal for **actions** and amber for **attention**. Never use amber for primary buttons; never use teal for warnings.

### Accessibility & contrast (WCAG AA)

Target: **4.5:1** for body text, **3:1** for large text (≥18.66px bold / ≥24px) and UI components.

| White `#FFFFFF` | Deep Harbor `#0B1F33` | ~16.8:1 | ✅ AAA |
| White `#FFFFFF` | Harbor Navy 700 `#123A5A` | ~10.9:1 | ✅ AAA |
| Ink `#1F2937` | Cloud `#F5F7FA` | ~13.4:1 | ✅ AAA — default body pairing |
| Slate `#64748B` | Cloud `#F5F7FA` | ~4.8:1 | ✅ AA — muted text only |
| Signal Teal `#0E9F8E` | White `#FFFFFF` | ~3.0:1 | ⚠️ AA large / UI only — not for small body text |
| Deep Harbor `#0B1F33` | Signal Teal `#0E9F8E` | ~5.6:1 | ✅ AA — use for button labels on teal |
| Ink `#1F2937` | Manifest Amber `#F2A93B` | ~8.5:1 | ✅ AAA — labels on amber |

| White `#FFFFFF` | Success `#16A34A` | ~3.3:1 | ⚠️ AA large / UI only |

**Rules:** use Deep Harbor text on teal and amber buttons, never white-on-teal for small labels. Keep amber and teal accents off small text on white; use them for fills, icons, borders, and badges instead.

## 6. Typography

| Role | Primary | Free alternatives (Google Fonts) |
|---|---|---|
| **Headings** | **Söhne** / **Inter Display** | **Inter**, **Manrope**, **Plus Jakarta Sans** |
| **Body** | **Inter** | **Inter**, **Public Sans**, **IBM Plex Sans** |
| **Mono (documents, HS codes, figures)** | **JetBrains Mono** | **JetBrains Mono**, **IBM Plex Mono**, **Roboto Mono** |

Pairing recommendation: **Inter** for headings and body (tight, neutral, highly legible) with **JetBrains Mono** for all data surfaces — HS codes, weights, quantities, document numbers, and totals. Monospace signals "this is a verifiable value."

## 7. Iconography

- **Style:** monoline, geometric, consistent 1.5–2px stroke (scaled to grid), 2px corner radius, round caps.
- **Grid:** draw on a 24px grid with a 2px safe padding; optical alignment over mathematical.
- **Colour:** single-colour by default (Ink or Deep Harbor); teal for active/selected; amber reserved for attention.
- **Metaphors to use:** documents and page corners, seals and stamps, checkmarks, routes/corridors, containers and pallets, HS-code tags, scales for weight, signatures.
- **Metaphors to avoid:** globes, handshakes, swooshes, generic "connection" node webs, currency symbols as decoration, cartoon ships/planes, emoji-style icons.
- **Consistency:** all icons must feel like one family — same stroke weight, same corner radius, same optical density. Provide filled variants only for active/selected states.

## 8. Imagery & Illustration

**Photography direction**

- Documentary and real: African ports, container yards, customs offices, warehouses, trading markets, and working professionals — not staged models.
- Natural light, honest colour, shallow depth of field. Prefer real Nigerian and East African contexts.
- Show hands, documents, screens, and cargo in use. Candid over posed.

**Illustration direction**

- Precise line-based illustration echoing the iconography system — thin strokes, geometric forms, restrained fills.
- Use Cloud, Deep Harbor, and a single teal/amber accent. Illustrate processes and document flows, not abstract "connection" graphics.
- Flat, editorial, and calm — no gradients, no 3D, no isometric clichés.

**Avoid:** generic stock handshakes, globes, smiling call-centre headshots, glossy 3D renders, and anything that reads as "template corporate."

## 10. Do's and Don'ts

| ✅ Do | ❌ Don't |
|---|---|
| Use Deep Harbor as the foundation and teal for action. | Don't use teal or amber as large background fills. |
| Keep typography calm, tight, and highly legible. | Don't mix more than two type families per surface. |
| Use monospace for HS codes, amounts, and document numbers. | Don't set body copy or headings in monospace. |
| Keep icons monoline, geometric, and one family. | Don't use globes, handshakes, swooshes, or node webs. |
| Show real African trade contexts in imagery. | Don't use staged stock handshakes or glossy 3D renders. |
| Write precise, calm, specific copy. | Don't use hype, exclamation marks, or vague superlatives. |
| Verify contrast (AA) for all text. | Don't put white text on teal/success at small sizes. |
| Reserve amber for attention and status. | Don't use amber for primary buttons. |
